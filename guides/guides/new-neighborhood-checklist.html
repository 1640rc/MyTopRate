Just Moved? The Businesses to Find First

Last updated: September 2026

Moving means losing your entire mental map at once. The pharmacy you knew, the guy who cut your hair, the place you ordered from on bad days — all gone, replaced by a list of names you've never heard.

You don't need to solve all of it. You need about eight businesses, and they're worth different amounts of effort.

Week one: the ones you'll need immediately

These are low-stakes and interchangeable. Don't overthink them — pick the closest option rated above 4.0 and move on. You can always change later.

  • Grocery store. Convenience wins. You'll go weekly and distance compounds.
  • Pharmacy. Same logic, plus check the hours — a 24-hour one nearby is worth knowing about before you need it at 11pm.
  • Coffee. Try three in the first month rather than researching one.
  • A reliable takeout place. You want one decent option locked in for the night when you have no energy. Sort by rating, pick something with high review volume, done.
The rule for week one

Nearest acceptable option beats best option. You're building a baseline, not optimizing.

Month one: the ones worth actual research

These are where a bad choice costs real money or real hassle, so spend the time.

A mechanic

Find one before your car breaks, because choosing under pressure is how people end up overpaying. Look for reviews mentioning that estimates held firm and that the shop declined unnecessary work. Read the 3-star reviews.

A dentist and a doctor

Check insurance first — that constrains the list more than ratings do. Among what's covered, read for wait times, billing surprises, and whether people feel rushed.

A barber or salon

Read for individual names and book a specific person. Expect to try two before you find the right one; that's normal and not a research failure.

An urgent care

Know where it is and what hours it keeps before you need it. Look up the nearest emergency room too. Ten minutes now, potentially valuable later.

Ongoing: the fun part

Restaurants are the one category where you shouldn't settle quickly. Finding the good ones is most of what makes a new place feel like home.

A method that works: pick one category a week and search it properly. Pizza this week, tacos next, a proper sit-down dinner the week after. Sort by rating, filter for real review volume, go to the top one. You'll build a genuine list in two months instead of eating at the same three places for a year.

Reading ratings in a place you don't know yet

One thing to watch: you don't have a feel for the local baseline yet. In some neighborhoods the best restaurant around is a 4.4. In others, 4.4 is unremarkable.

So look at the whole result list before judging any single number. If everything nearby sits between 3.9 and 4.4, then 4.4 is the good one, and holding out for a 4.7 means never going anywhere.

Also be wary of relying on your old city's price instincts. Both ratings and price symbols are relative to the local area.

The one thing ratings can't do

Ratings will get you to competent businesses. They won't tell you which coffee shop you'll end up going to every Saturday, or which restaurant will become the one you take visitors to. That part needs actually going places.

Use the research to avoid wasting time on bad options, then let the good ones find you.
