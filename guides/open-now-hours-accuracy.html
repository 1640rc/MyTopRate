"Open Now" Is Wrong More Often Than You'd Think

Last updated: September 2026

You drive fifteen minutes to a place that said it was open. The lights are off. There's a handwritten sign on the door. This happens to everyone, and it's not really Google's fault.

Where those hours come from

Business hours online aren't measured. Nobody is checking whether the door is unlocked. They're typed in by the business owner, usually once, often years ago, and then left alone.

Sometimes other sources fill them in — a listing from a directory, information scraped from the business's own website, or suggestions submitted by users. Those can be stale too.

So "Open now" really means "according to a schedule someone entered at some point, this should be open." That's usually right. It's wrong often enough to be worth a phone call.

Rule of thumb

The more it would cost you to arrive at a locked door, the more it's worth a 20-second phone call first.

When hours are least reliable

Holidays, and the days around them

This is by far the biggest failure mode. Regular weekly hours are usually accurate. Holiday hours require the owner to remember to set a special schedule, and most don't. The day before and after a holiday are nearly as bad.

Small independent businesses

A one-person barber shop or a family restaurant closes when someone's sick, when there's a family event, or when it's slow. None of that reaches the internet. Chains and larger operations have staff whose job includes keeping listings current.

Kitchens vs. the building

A restaurant listed as open until midnight may stop serving food at 10:30. The bar stays open. If you're going specifically to eat, "open" and "still cooking" are different questions.

Recently opened or recently changed

New businesses, businesses that just moved, and businesses under new ownership all tend to have listings that lag behind reality by months.

Seasonal places

Anywhere with summer hours, winter hours, or a slow season. The listing frequently shows whichever schedule was entered most recently, regardless of the current month.

Signals that the hours are probably right

Some listings are well maintained, and there are tells:

  • The business replies to reviews. Someone is paying attention to the listing. Their hours are probably current too.
  • There are recent photos posted by the business. Same signal.
  • Google shows "popular times" or live busy-ness data. That's derived from actual phone location data, not from what the owner typed. If it says the place is busy right now, somebody is genuinely inside.
  • Recent reviews mention visiting at that hour. A review from last week describing a late dinner is better evidence than the posted closing time.

The 20-second check

Before any trip you'd be annoyed to waste:

  1. Look at the "popular times" graph — is there activity right now?
  2. Glance at the newest two or three reviews for anything about hours or closures.
  3. If it's a holiday, a Sunday, or a small independent place, just call. It takes less time than reading this paragraph.

Calling feels old-fashioned and it remains the single most reliable method available. A human answering the phone is proof the door is open.

How we handle it

MyTopRate shows the open/closed status Google reports, and the "Open now only" filter uses that same data. It's the best information available through the API, and it comes with all the caveats above — we're passing along what Google has, not independently verifying that anyone unlocked the door this morning.

That's why the filter is a toggle rather than something applied automatically. Sometimes you'd rather see a great place that's closed right now and go tomorrow, than settle for a mediocre one that happens to be open.

Use "Open now only" to narrow things down when you're heading out immediately. Turn it off when you're planning ahead — otherwise you're hiding good options for no reason.
